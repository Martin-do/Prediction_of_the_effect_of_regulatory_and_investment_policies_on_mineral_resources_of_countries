# Scope and claims guardrail

## What the outcome measures

The World Bank annual inward FDI net-inflow series measures total inward foreign direct investment at the national level. It captures the investment environment into which resource projects must fit, but it does not identify the industrial sector receiving the investment.

## Defensible manuscript scope

**Signal admissibility for AI-MCDM decision support in resource-rich economies.**

The resource context is supplied by oil-rent exposure and by prespecified oil-rent-intensive country universes. The outcome remains aggregate national FDI.

## Defensible claims

- validation setup changes the apparent usefulness of candidate signals;
- oil-rent and Regulatory Quality verdicts vary across targets, thresholds, and validation geometries;
- aggregate FDI can be used as a national investment-context outcome;
- signals should be classified before being used as empirically supported AI-MCDM criteria;
- subgroup evidence is conditional, not causal.

## Claims that are not permitted

- prediction of petroleum-sector investment;
- prediction of mining-sector investment;
- prediction of Nigerian tar-sand project investment;
- causal effects of governance or oil rents;
- sector-level policy prescriptions from aggregate FDI;
- direct translation of predictive importance into MCDM weights.

## Standalone title direction

**Before the Weights: How Validation Design Changes Signal-Admissibility Verdicts for AI-MCDM in Resource-Rich Economies**

This title reflects the actual outcome and does not require knowledge of any other manuscript.
